Something came back after the fact:

$ARGUMENTS

1. **Find the question.** Read `wiki/questions/INDEX.md`, then grep `wiki/questions/` for
   the nouns and for `aliases:`. Nobody says "q-0042" in a corridor — they say "that
   Avios thing". Search the words they used, not the id.
2. If more than one question could match, **ask which.** Filing an outcome against the
   wrong question is worse than not filing it at all.
3. Append a dated entry to `## Outcome log` in that record: what happened, who says so,
   and what it changes. Never overwrite an earlier entry.
4. **Then follow it through** — an outcome is rarely just a note:
   - a design that ran → amend the method's `evidence:` and record what actually happened
   - an estimate contradicted → note it on the concept, and write a `wiki/traps/` page if
     the mistake would be made again
   - a decision taken → record it, because the next question about this area will ask
     what we decided last time
   - a skill that has now been used twice → say so; that is the promotion bar
5. If the question is already `concluded`, leave it concluded and append anyway.
   **Records stay open to new information after they close.** A question that concluded in
   September and was proved wrong in November should read that way.
